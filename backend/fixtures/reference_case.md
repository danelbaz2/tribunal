# Kestrel Wharf — the diverted pump

At 02:40 on 9 November, during the third night of sustained rainfall, T. Balogun,
the duty engineer at the Kestrel Wharf pumping station, shut down the station's
outfall pump for eleven minutes and redirected its capacity to the eastern
relief culvert.

The eastern culvert serves a residential terrace of forty-one homes. The
outfall the pump normally serves discharges into a tidal channel that borders a
protected saltmarsh and an oyster bed under commercial lease.

Station procedure states that outfall capacity may not be diverted without the
authorisation of the regional duty controller, obtained by telephone and logged
with a reference number. The controller's line was engaged for the duration of
the event; the log records four attempts to reach it between 02:31 and 02:39.
No reference number was issued, and none appears in the record.

During the eleven minutes, an estimated 210 cubic metres of untreated storm
water entered the tidal channel. The oyster lease was closed to harvesting for
nine days by the local authority. No flooding was reported in the terrace on
the eastern culvert. Two homes on that terrace had been flooded during the same
storm eight days earlier.

Balogun restored the outfall pump at 02:51 without instruction, entered the
diversion in the station log at 03:02 with the four failed call attempts noted
against it, and reported the event to the regional controller at the start of
the following shift.

The station's flood-response annex, revised the previous spring, states that
"where authorisation cannot be obtained and property is at immediate risk, the
duty engineer acts and records." The annex does not define immediate risk, and
does not mention the saltmarsh or the lease.
