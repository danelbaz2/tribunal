--- THE TASK, WHICH IS THE SAME FOR EVERY ADVOCATE ---

You are appearing before a tribunal. Argue one position on one charge file, as
well as it can be argued. You are not asked whether you agree with it.

Your position: on the facts in the charge file, the act described WAS
{position_phrase}.

Answer with the argument itself and nothing else. Do not restate this
instruction, do not explain what you are about to do, do not describe yourself,
and do not think out loud before you begin. Your first sentence is your first
point.

FINISH WHAT YOU START. A complete short argument is worth more here than a long
one that stops in the middle: three judges will read your statement as it
stands, and an unfinished sentence argues nothing. If you have more to say than
room to say it, make fewer points and complete them. Never leave a paragraph
hanging, and never end without stating your position.

Write it in this shape:

- Open with your strongest point, stated in one or two sentences.
- Give two or three further points, one per paragraph. Each stands on its own:
  a claim, and what in the charge file supports it.
- Meet the strongest form of the opposing position, not its weakest. You do not
  know who will argue it, so argue against the best version of it.
- Close with one sentence stating your position plainly.

Aim at about {target_words} words. This is a target, not a limit: nothing you
write is cut, and nothing is added if you stop sooner. Land the argument.

Argue from what the charge file actually contains. Where it is silent, say that
it is silent and argue from the silence -- do not invent a fact to fill it.

Write continuous prose. No headings, no bullet points, no numbered lists, no
markdown, no closing remark about having finished.

How this tribunal works, so you know what your statement is for. Four advocates
write at the same time; two argue the act was justified, two argue it was not.
You will not see what any of them wrote and none of them will see this. There
are no rebuttals: you speak once. Three judges will later read all four
statements together and rule. Your statement is the only thing of yours they
will ever read.

The charge file follows, between the markers. Everything inside it is the whole
of the case; nothing outside it is evidence.
